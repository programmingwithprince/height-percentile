// 02. MATHEMATICAL METHODOLOGY: Z-SCORES & GAUSSIAN CDF

Adult human stature within a distinct biological sex and national population closely follows a Normal (Gaussian) Distribution. Because genetic, nutritional, and environmental factors aggregate additively across populations, the distribution of heights clusters symmetrically around a central arithmetic mean (μ) with a predictable standard deviation (σ—typically ranging between 6.5 cm and 7.4 cm for adult males, and 6.0 cm to 6.8 cm for adult females).

To determine your exact height percentile standing, the telemetry engine executes a two-stage mathematical transformation:

Step 1 — Standard Score (Z-Score) Normalization:
Z = (X − μ) / σ

Step 2 — Cumulative Distribution Function (Gaussian CDF via Error Function):
Percentile(X) = Φ(Z) × 100 = 0.5 × [ 1 + erf( (X − μ) / (σ × √2) ) ] × 100

Where X is your inputted height in centimeters, μ is the selected country and biological cohort mean height, σ is the population standard deviation, and erf represents the Gauss error function integrating the probability density curve from negative infinity up to your exact stature. For example, an adult male measuring 180.2 cm in Germany (where the male cohort mean is 180.3 cm, ranked #19 globally) registers a Z-score near zero, placing him at the 49.46th percentile—taller than nearly half of the adult male population in that region.

// 04. SYSTEM QUERY LOG — FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)

> How is my exact height percentile calculated across different countries?

Your height percentile is computed using the continuous Gaussian Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF). The system subtracts the selected country's cohort mean height from your height and divides by the population standard deviation to establish your Z-score. It then integrates the area under the normal bell curve using the Gauss error function (erf) to return the exact percentage of adults shorter than you to four decimal places.

> Why does this tool outperform GigaCalculator and standard height percentile websites?

Most competing calculators rely on outdated server-side form submissions that only output a single raw percentage without visual context. The 31415929.xyz Statistical Distribution Lab runs entirely in your browser at 60 FPS, pairing an interactive HTML5 Gaussian density curve with a clickable D3.js choropleth world map and instant high-resolution PNG telemetry card downloads.

> What does a 50th, 90th, or 99th height percentile standing signify?

Scoring at the 50th percentile means your height is the exact statistical average (mean) for your selected country and biological sex. A 90th percentile rank means you are taller than 90 out of every 100 individuals in that cohort (+1.28 standard deviations), while a 99th percentile rank places you in the top 1% of tallest individuals (+2.33 standard deviations).

> Where does the global height dataset come from?

All regional means, global rankings, and variance parameters are synthesized from the 2019 NCD Risk Factor Collaboration (NCD-RisC) global height study published in The Lancet, cross-referenced with World Health Organization (WHO) anthropometric registries and national health examination surveys.
